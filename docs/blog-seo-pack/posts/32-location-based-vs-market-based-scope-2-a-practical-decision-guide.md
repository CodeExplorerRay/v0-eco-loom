# Location-Based vs Market-Based Scope 2: A Practical Decision Guide

Scope 2 Accounting | November 26, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Scope 2 Accounting
**Primary Keyword:** location-based vs market-based Scope 2
**Publish Date:** November 26, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Electricity reporting can produce more than one defensible view of emissions because the grid serving a location and the contractual instruments a company holds answer different questions. Teams often get stuck when they try to choose one number before understanding what each method represents.

The practical starting point is to identify the reporting framework and request that applies, then prepare the data needed to calculate each required view consistently.

## What the location-based view describes

A location-based calculation reflects the average emissions intensity of the electricity grid where consumption occurred. It is useful for understanding the broader grid context around a site and for comparing activity across locations when the same period and suitable factors are used.

This view depends on complete consumption data, correct site geography, and a factor that matches the reporting period and boundary. A utility bill without a reliable site mapping can undermine the result even when the arithmetic is correct.

## What the market-based view describes

A market-based calculation considers contractual arrangements associated with purchased electricity, where applicable. The quality and eligibility of instruments can depend on rules in the relevant market and reporting guidance. Teams should retain the contract or instrument evidence and document how it is applied.

Do not treat a certificate or supplier statement as self-explanatory. Record the quantity, period, geography, retirement or allocation evidence where relevant, and the method used to match it to consumption.

## Keep the two views traceable

Maintain a shared electricity ledger with site, meter, period, consumption, source, factor, and calculation method. Link contractual evidence to the same site and period instead of keeping it in a separate folder that cannot be reconciled to the inventory.

A review should check for missing sites, overlapping claims, mismatched periods, and unusual changes. It should also confirm that the team has not switched methods without recording why.

## Explain the result without implying more than it shows

When reporting both views, label them clearly and explain the role each plays. Avoid presenting a lower market-based result as proof that the physical grid serving a facility changed. Likewise, avoid ignoring contractual choices when they are material to the reporting question.

**Key Takeaway:** Location-based and market-based Scope 2 figures describe different aspects of electricity sourcing; preserve the inputs and label each view clearly.
