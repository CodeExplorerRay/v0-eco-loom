# Renewable Energy Certificates: What They Do and Don’t Prove

Renewable Electricity | November 27, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Renewable Electricity
**Primary Keyword:** renewable energy certificates
**Publish Date:** November 27, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Renewable energy certificates and similar energy attributes can help organizations document certain electricity purchasing claims. They are also easy to overstate. A certificate is evidence within a defined market and accounting system; it is not, by itself, proof that a particular facility received renewable electricity at every moment.

A careful process starts by asking what claim the instrument supports, what evidence is available, and which rules apply in the relevant market.

## Understand the claim before reviewing the certificate

A certificate may represent an attribute associated with a defined quantity of electricity generation. Its meaning depends on the instrument, market rules, reporting method, and how it is acquired and retired or otherwise allocated. Teams should avoid treating all certificates as interchangeable.

Write the intended claim in plain language before approving a purchase. A claim about annual matching is different from a claim about hourly supply or a direct physical connection.

## Check the evidence and matching details

Keep documentation for the instrument, supplier, generating source, quantity, period, geography, and retirement or allocation status where applicable. Reconcile the volume to the organization’s electricity consumption and the sites included in the claim.

If a provider supplies only a summary, ask for supporting records or a clear explanation of the chain of custody. Store the response with the reporting-period evidence so it can be reviewed later.

## Separate accounting treatment from physical impact

A certificate may affect a market-based accounting result under applicable rules, but that result should not be confused with the physical characteristics of the local grid. Nor should the purchase alone be described as causing a new renewable project unless the organization has evidence supporting that broader claim.

The distinction matters because stakeholders may interpret “powered by renewable energy” as a statement about actual supply. Be precise about the boundary and time period.

## Review claims before publication

Ask sustainability, legal, procurement, and communications reviewers to assess the evidence together. They should verify that the wording matches the instrument and that no location or consumption was counted twice.

Where the evidence is limited, narrow the language. A qualified, supportable statement is more durable than a sweeping claim that cannot be explained.

**Key Takeaway:** Renewable energy certificates support specific, rules-dependent claims; verify the instrument and match the public wording to what the records actually show.
