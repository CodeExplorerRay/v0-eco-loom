# How to Calculate Business Travel Emissions From Imperfect Expense Data

Business Travel | November 28, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Business Travel
**Primary Keyword:** business travel emissions
**Publish Date:** November 28, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Business travel records rarely arrive in the exact shape a carbon inventory needs. Expense systems may show a reimbursement amount but not a route, while booking platforms may capture ticket details without a clean link to the employee or reporting period.

The answer is not to wait for perfect data. It is to define what the current sources can support, identify the largest blind spots, and improve collection in a deliberate order.

## List the travel streams first

Separate air, rail, rental vehicles, taxis, mileage reimbursements, and lodging where those activities are relevant to the inventory. The sources and assumptions differ, so combining everything into one expense total too early hides what is and is not represented.

Record the systems that hold each stream, the owner who can export it, the available fields, and the reporting period. This simple map usually reveals duplicate channels and missing regions.

## Use the strongest available activity data

A travel record with origin and destination generally supports a more specific estimate than a reimbursement amount alone. Where route detail is unavailable, use a documented proxy that is appropriate to the category and available guidance. Do not imply that a spend-based estimate has the same precision as distance-based activity.

Keep the unit, method, factor source, and any assumptions beside each result. That makes it possible to improve the estimate later without losing the history of how it was prepared.

## Reconcile sources and avoid double counting

Compare booking-platform records with expense exports and travel-management reports. A booked ticket may also appear as an employee reimbursement, and a mileage payment may be included in a fleet file. Decide which source is primary for each activity and how exceptions are handled.

Use targeted checks: duplicate transaction IDs, missing itinerary details, unusual month-to-month changes, and trips recorded outside the expected period.

## Improve the process without overburdening travelers

Ask travel or finance teams to add a few high-value fields at booking or export time rather than launching an open-ended employee survey. Explain why the information is collected and who can access it. Review data quality by region and source so one weak channel does not become invisible in the total.

**Key Takeaway:** Imperfect travel data can support a useful estimate when the team separates streams, documents proxies, and reconciles overlapping sources.
