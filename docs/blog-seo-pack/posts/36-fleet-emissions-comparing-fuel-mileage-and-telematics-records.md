# Fleet Emissions: Comparing Fuel, Mileage, and Telematics Records

Fleet Data | November 30, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Fleet Data
**Primary Keyword:** fleet emissions data
**Publish Date:** November 30, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Fleet emissions calculations can draw on fuel purchases, odometer readings, fuel-card statements, maintenance systems, or telematics. Each source offers a different view of vehicle activity, and none should be assumed complete simply because it is digital.

The best source depends on the question, the fleet, and the reliability of the records. A practical inventory documents how sources fit together and where they do not.

## Build a vehicle and activity register

Start with a current list of owned, leased, and otherwise operated vehicles that are relevant to the inventory. Include vehicle identifier, fuel type, operating unit, and in-service dates. Keep a clear process for additions, disposals, temporary replacements, and vehicles shared across teams.

Map each vehicle to the systems that record fuel or distance. Missing vehicles and inconsistent identifiers are common causes of unexplained changes.

## Understand what each source measures

Fuel-card records can show purchased volume, but may include non-fleet purchases or omit fuel bought through other channels. Mileage can support activity estimates, but odometer readings may be irregular or incomplete. Telematics can offer frequent data, yet device coverage and vehicle assignment may vary.

For every source, note units, coverage, timing, and known gaps. Do not combine values until the team understands whether they refer to the same vehicles and period.

## Reconcile before calculating

Compare fuel and mileage trends by vehicle or fleet group. Investigate records with unusually high consumption, missing months, duplicate transactions, or a sudden change in vehicle count. Use maintenance records or dispatch logs to understand anomalies where available.

If one source is used to fill gaps in another, write down the rule. Apply it consistently and flag estimates rather than hiding them in the total.

## Make improvement part of fleet operations

The carbon process can reveal operational data problems that also affect cost and maintenance. Work with fleet managers to improve vehicle identifiers, fuel coding, and return-of-vehicle procedures. Review completeness each month rather than discovering missing records at year end.

**Key Takeaway:** Reliable fleet emissions data comes from a reconciled vehicle register and a documented understanding of what fuel, mileage, and telematics records each capture.
