# Refrigerants and Fugitive Emissions: A Facility Data Checklist

Facility Emissions | December 1, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Facility Emissions
**Primary Keyword:** refrigerant emissions tracking
**Publish Date:** December 1, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Refrigerant losses can be easy to overlook because the relevant evidence may sit in maintenance records rather than utility or fuel files. A facility may know that equipment was serviced without having a consistent way to connect the service event to refrigerant type, quantity, and reporting period.

A practical tracking process brings facilities, maintenance, and sustainability teams together around a small set of repeatable records.

## Identify equipment and owners

Create a register of relevant cooling, refrigeration, and other equipment, with facility location, equipment identifier, refrigerant type where known, and responsible maintenance provider. Note when equipment is added, replaced, or retired.

The register does not need to start as a perfect technical database. It needs enough structure for a team to locate the source record and determine whether an event belongs in the inventory.

## Capture service and replenishment events

Ask service providers to record the equipment ID, date, refrigerant type, amount added or removed, and whether the work involved repair, maintenance, or replacement. Keep invoices and service tickets with the structured record rather than relying on an annual total from memory.

Where a quantity is estimated or missing, flag it. Do not treat an equipment capacity as if it were an actual loss unless the chosen method explicitly supports that approach.

## Reconcile records across facilities

Compare service logs, contractor invoices, purchase records, and equipment registers. Look for events with missing facility identifiers, mismatched units, or duplicate tickets. Confirm that a service provider’s consolidated invoice does not combine multiple sites without a usable breakdown.

A monthly exception list can help facilities resolve missing details while the work is still recent.

## Keep assumptions and factors visible

Use the calculation method and emissions factors required by the applicable inventory guidance. Record the factor source, version, and any assumptions used to convert units or estimate gaps. Keep that documentation with the facility’s evidence so future reviewers can reproduce the result.

**Key Takeaway:** Refrigerant tracking becomes more reliable when equipment, service events, quantities, and calculation assumptions stay connected in one reviewable trail.
