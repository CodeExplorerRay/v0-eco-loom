# Freight Emissions: What to Capture From Carriers and 3PLs

Freight Data | December 4, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Freight Data
**Primary Keyword:** freight emissions data
**Publish Date:** December 4, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Freight activity can move through carriers, brokers, third-party logistics providers, and company-managed fleets. Each party may hold a different piece of the shipment record. Without a shared structure, teams can receive a total that looks complete but cannot be traced to routes, modes, or reporting periods.

The most useful first step is to define what shipment-level information is available and who can provide it consistently.

## Define the shipment boundary

Clarify which freight movements are relevant to the inventory and how the organization distinguishes inbound, outbound, inter-site, and customer-arranged shipments. The answer may depend on the reporting method and commercial arrangements, so document the chosen rule rather than assuming every shipment is treated the same way.

Include the owner for each stream: logistics, procurement, a carrier, or a 3PL. Record which movements are not visible yet.

## Request practical fields from providers

A useful data request may include shipment identifier, origin and destination, mode, distance or route, weight, service type, dates, and subcontracted legs where available. Ask providers to explain any allocation method used for shared loads and how empty or return movements are handled.

Avoid requesting an elaborate format that the provider cannot maintain. Agree on a stable export, definitions, and delivery cadence first.

## Validate before applying factors

Check units, missing routes, duplicate shipment IDs, and unexpected changes in total weight or distance. Compare provider totals to internal freight spend or shipment counts as a reasonableness check, not as a substitute for activity evidence.

If a route includes multiple modes, preserve the legs when the data supports it. If a leg is estimated, mark it so reviewers can distinguish reported activity from a proxy.

## Use data gaps to improve visibility

Rank missing information by volume and decision relevance. A team may begin with a high-volume lane or one carrier that serves many sites, then expand once the request process works. Track provider coverage and quality each cycle so “data received” does not become the only measure of progress.

**Key Takeaway:** Freight data is more useful when shipment boundaries, provider fields, route legs, and validation checks are defined before calculations begin.
