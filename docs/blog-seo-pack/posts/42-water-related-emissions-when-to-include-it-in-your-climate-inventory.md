# Water-Related Emissions: When to Include It in Your Climate Inventory

Water and Climate Data | December 6, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Water and Climate Data
**Primary Keyword:** water-related emissions
**Publish Date:** December 6, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Water use is important for many organizations, but it does not automatically belong in every greenhouse gas inventory in the same way. Teams should distinguish direct emissions from water or wastewater operations, indirect emissions associated with energy or treatment, and broader water impacts that are tracked through separate environmental metrics.

The right approach depends on the inventory boundary, applicable guidance, operational context, and the decisions the data should support.

## Identify the activity before selecting a factor

Start with the physical process: water withdrawn, purchased water, pumping, on-site treatment, wastewater discharge, or another activity. These are not interchangeable records. A water invoice may show volume but not the energy used to move or treat it.

Map the source, facility, period, unit, and owner. Then determine whether the activity is relevant to the emissions inventory or better handled in a parallel water-management program.

## Look for material operational connections

Water-related energy can matter at sites that pump, heat, cool, desalinate, or treat large volumes. Industrial processes may also have direct emissions associated with treatment or discharge, depending on the activity and methodology. Avoid assuming that every cubic meter has the same climate profile across locations.

Talk with facilities and process owners to understand where energy and water records connect. A utility bill and a water meter may need to be reviewed together.

## Keep climate and water metrics distinct

Water scarcity, withdrawal, consumption, and greenhouse gas emissions answer different questions. A company can improve one metric while another moves in a different direction. Keep definitions, units, and boundaries separate so a climate indicator does not get presented as a complete measure of water stewardship.

If teams use a proxy or regional factor, preserve the source and explain what the estimate represents. Do not imply site-level precision when the input is an average.

## Decide what to track next

Prioritize water-related emissions where the activity could influence operational choices or where the current data reveals a material gap. Improve the underlying meter and energy records before adding detailed calculations that the team cannot maintain.

**Key Takeaway:** Include water-related emissions when the activity and method support a meaningful climate estimate, and keep those results distinct from broader water-impact metrics.
