# Environmental Product Declarations vs. Product Carbon Footprints

Product Environmental Data | December 8, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Product Environmental Data
**Primary Keyword:** EPD vs product carbon footprint
**Publish Date:** December 8, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Environmental Product Declarations and product carbon footprints are related ways of communicating product impacts, but they are not interchangeable labels. A product carbon footprint focuses on greenhouse gas emissions within a defined method and boundary. An EPD is generally a standardized declaration that may cover multiple environmental indicators and follows program-specific rules.

The exact scope depends on the product category, applicable standards, and program operator. Teams should read the underlying documentation rather than infer comparability from the document title.

## Compare purpose and scope first

Ask what decision the information will support. A buyer may request a carbon footprint for a specific product comparison, while a project specification may call for an EPD prepared under a particular product category rule. The request determines which boundaries and indicators matter.

Check the functional unit, life-cycle stages, geography, data period, and product version. Two documents about similar products may still represent different scopes.

## Understand the declaration requirements

An EPD is commonly prepared under program rules and may involve review or verification, but requirements vary. Confirm the program, applicable category rules, validity dates, and any stated limitations. Do not assume every EPD has the same coverage or assurance level.

For a carbon footprint, confirm the calculation method, included stages, factor sources, and whether the result is independently reviewed. A concise summary should point readers to these details.

## Avoid comparing unlike results

Comparisons are more meaningful when the products serve the same function and the studies use compatible boundaries and assumptions. If they do not, explain the differences or avoid ranking the products. A single lower figure does not prove a product is environmentally preferable across all impacts.

Keep versions and supporting reports linked to the product configuration they describe. When materials, suppliers, or manufacturing sites change, assess whether the published result still represents the product being sold.

## Use the document as evidence, not a shortcut

A declaration can support procurement or communications work, but the organization still needs to understand what the evidence says. Ask technical and sustainability reviewers to confirm the claim fits the documented scope.

**Key Takeaway:** An EPD and a product carbon footprint may answer different questions; compare their scope, rules, and units before using either as evidence.
