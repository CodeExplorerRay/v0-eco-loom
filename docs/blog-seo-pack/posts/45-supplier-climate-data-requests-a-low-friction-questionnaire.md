# Supplier Climate Data Requests: A Low-Friction Questionnaire

Supplier Engagement | December 9, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Supplier Engagement
**Primary Keyword:** supplier emissions questionnaire
**Publish Date:** December 9, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Supplier climate questionnaires often fail before the answers are reviewed. Requests can be too broad, use unfamiliar terms, or arrive without explaining why the information matters. Suppliers then return partial responses, and procurement teams spend the next month chasing context.

A better request is short, specific, and connected to a clear follow-up plan. It should ask only for information that the buyer can use and explain how the data will be handled.

## Begin with a defined use case

Decide whether the request supports an inventory estimate, product assessment, supplier engagement plan, or another defined purpose. That choice shapes the data fields and the level of detail needed. Avoid asking every supplier for a full emissions inventory if the immediate need is a basic screening input.

Segment suppliers by spend, category, risk, or strategic relevance so the request can be proportionate.

## Ask questions suppliers can answer

A first request might ask whether the supplier has a GHG inventory, which entities and periods it covers, whether the result was reviewed, and which source documents or methodology summary are available. If product-specific data is needed, state the product, unit, boundary, and period clearly.

Define terms and allow “not available” or “not applicable” responses. A structured explanation is more useful than a forced guess.

## Make the process easy to complete

Use one stable channel and provide a named contact for questions. Keep the form accessible, allow answers to be saved, and avoid requesting information the company already holds. Explain deadlines and whether responses will be shared or aggregated.

Coordinate the request with procurement so the supplier does not receive overlapping questionnaires from different teams.

## Close the loop with suppliers

Track response status, data quality, and unresolved questions. Share a concise explanation of how the information may influence engagement or estimates. Where the supplier cannot respond, offer a practical next step rather than treating silence as a final answer.

Improve the questionnaire after each cycle, but keep core questions stable enough to compare responses over time.

**Key Takeaway:** A supplier climate request works better when it has a specific purpose, plain-language questions, one owner, and a clear follow-up path.
