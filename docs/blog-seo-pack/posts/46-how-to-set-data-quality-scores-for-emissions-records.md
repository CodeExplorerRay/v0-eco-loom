# How to Set Data Quality Scores for Emissions Records

Emissions Data Quality | December 10, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Emissions Data Quality
**Primary Keyword:** emissions data quality score
**Publish Date:** December 10, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

A data quality score can help teams decide which emissions records need review first. It can also create false confidence if the score is too complicated, hides weak inputs, or is treated as a grade for the people who submitted the data.

The score should make uncertainty easier to discuss. It should not replace a clear explanation of the evidence and method behind a result.

## Choose dimensions that matter to the calculation

A practical model may consider source reliability, measurement specificity, time-period fit, geographic or operational representativeness, and completeness. Pick dimensions that help distinguish strong records from estimates in the organization’s actual workflow.

Define each dimension in plain language. A score of “high” should mean the same thing to a facilities manager, analyst, and reviewer.

## Keep the scale small and consistent

A three- or five-level scale is often easier to apply than a long numerical rubric. Provide examples for each level, such as a metered quantity with a matching invoice versus a broad proxy with limited documentation. Make sure the score reflects the input and method, not how polished the final spreadsheet looks.

If different categories need different criteria, explain why and avoid comparing scores that were built on incompatible scales.

## Separate data quality from emissions magnitude

A large emissions estimate is not necessarily high quality, and a small estimate is not automatically low quality. Keep confidence and magnitude as separate fields so teams do not overlook a major but uncertain category or overreact to a well-measured minor source.

Use the score to guide review priority, data requests, and sensitivity checks. Record any override and the reason for it.

## Test the scoring model with real records

Apply the rubric to a sample from different facilities and data types. Ask reviewers whether scores are reproducible and whether they point toward useful actions. If two reviewers routinely disagree, the definitions need examples or clearer boundaries.

Revisit the model when sources or methods change, and keep prior scores when you need to explain how data maturity improved.

**Key Takeaway:** Emissions data quality scores are useful when criteria are simple, reproducible, and kept separate from the size of the reported impact.
