# Emissions Factors: Choosing, Updating, and Documenting Them

Emissions Methodology | December 11, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Emissions Methodology
**Primary Keyword:** emissions factors
**Publish Date:** December 11, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

An emissions factor connects an activity quantity to an estimated greenhouse gas result. Choosing the factor is not a clerical step: its geography, time period, technology, gas coverage, and unit all affect how well it represents the activity.

A repeatable factor-management process helps teams make defensible choices without turning each reporting cycle into a fresh research project.

## Match the factor to the activity

Start with the activity data and identify the factor characteristics that matter. A factor for one fuel, grid, transport mode, or material may not fit another simply because the names look similar. Confirm the unit and conversion basis before applying it.

Use the source hierarchy required by the relevant methodology or reporting framework. When a preferred source is unavailable, document the alternative and why it was selected.

## Record enough detail to reproduce the calculation

Store factor name, source, publication or version date, geography, unit, applicable period, and any conversions. Link the factor to the records where it is used. Avoid copying a number into a spreadsheet without preserving where it came from.

A factor library can help, but it needs an owner. The owner should review changes, manage aliases, and prevent two teams from using different versions without realizing it.

## Set a controlled update policy

New factor releases do not automatically mean every prior calculation must be rewritten. Define when updates apply, how the team handles restatements, and whether a calculation uses the latest factor or a period-specific version. Follow applicable reporting guidance and document the decision.

Keep old factor versions available for reproducibility. A controlled change log should show what changed, who approved it, and which reporting periods were affected.

## Review units and sensitivity

Before sign-off, test unit conversions, factor selection, and calculation logic with a sample record. For high-impact categories with uncertain factors, consider whether a sensitivity range would help decision-makers understand the possible effect.

Explain uncertainty honestly. A precise-looking output is not evidence that the underlying factor is exact.

**Key Takeaway:** Good factor management ties each value to its source, unit, geography, period, and version so calculations can be reviewed and reproduced.
