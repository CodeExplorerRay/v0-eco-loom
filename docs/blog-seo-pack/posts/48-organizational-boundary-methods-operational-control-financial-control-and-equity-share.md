# Organizational Boundary Methods: Operational Control, Financial Control, and Equity Share

An organizational boundary method determines how a company consolidates emissions from entities and operations it owns or participates in. Common approaches include equity share, financial control, and operational control, but the right choice depends on the reporting framework and the organization’s circumstances.

Teams should not pick a method because it produces the easiest data collection. They should understand the method, apply it consistently, and document difficult cases.

## What the methods are intended to distinguish

Equity share generally reflects an organization’s economic interest in an operation. Control approaches focus on whether the company has financial or operational control under the applicable definitions. These descriptions are only a starting point; detailed interpretation should follow the relevant GHG accounting guidance and professional advice where needed.

A company with joint ventures, leases, franchises, or outsourced operations may need careful analysis rather than a simple ownership list.

## Build an entity decision register

List entities and significant operating arrangements, then record the facts used to assess each one: ownership, governance rights, who directs operations, and who controls relevant policies. Capture the conclusion, rationale, evidence, reviewer, and date.

This register helps teams avoid re-litigating the same issue each year and makes changes visible when contracts or ownership structures evolve.

## Translate the method into a collection plan

Once the boundary is set, map included operations to data owners and expected activity records. Make clear how shared sites and outsourced services are handled. A boundary rule that never reaches the intake checklist will be applied inconsistently in practice.

Keep excluded operations visible too. A reviewer should be able to understand the scope without guessing which entities were omitted.

## Reassess when the organization changes

Acquisitions, divestitures, new leases, and governance changes can affect consolidation decisions. Define an event-triggered review process so the boundary is updated when material changes occur, not only during annual reporting.

**Key Takeaway:** Boundary methods are useful only when the chosen approach is applied consistently to documented entity facts and translated into data ownership.
