# Base Year Recalculation: What Should Trigger a Reset?

Emissions Baselines | December 13, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Emissions Baselines
**Primary Keyword:** base year recalculation
**Publish Date:** December 13, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

A base year gives teams a reference point for tracking emissions change. If the organization changes its boundary, corrects a significant error, or improves its method, the historical comparison may no longer be like-for-like. That does not mean every change requires a recalculation.

A clear policy helps the company treat similar events consistently and explain why a trend line changed.

## Define the policy before a major change occurs

Document which events may trigger review, such as structural changes, significant methodological improvements, or discovery of material errors. The threshold and treatment should follow applicable accounting guidance and the organization’s reporting commitments.

A policy written after a difficult result appears can look selective. Agree on the decision process before the next acquisition or factor update creates pressure.

## Assess significance and comparability

Estimate how a change affects the base year and current period. Consider whether the reported trend would be meaningfully different if the issue were corrected. Keep calculations and assumptions so a reviewer can reproduce the assessment.

A change in data quality is not always a structural change. Improving current-period coverage may call for clearer disclosure or a better process rather than an automatic reset of the baseline.

## Preserve the original and recalculated views

If a recalculation is required, keep the original reported number, the revised number, and the reason for the adjustment. Show the affected entities, categories, periods, and methods. Do not silently overwrite the old baseline in a way that makes prior reports impossible to reconcile.

A change log should identify who approved the restatement and how it was communicated to stakeholders.

## Keep reduction claims aligned with the baseline

Targets and progress claims depend on a stable comparison. Explain which base year is used, whether recalculations occurred, and why. If the organization changes its boundary or method, communicate the effect separately from actual operational reductions where possible.

**Key Takeaway:** A base-year policy should define triggers, assess materiality consistently, and preserve both original and recalculated records with a clear explanation.
