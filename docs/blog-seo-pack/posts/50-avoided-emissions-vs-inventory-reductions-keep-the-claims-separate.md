# Avoided Emissions vs. Inventory Reductions: Keep the Claims Separate

Climate Impact Claims | December 14, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Climate Impact Claims
**Primary Keyword:** avoided emissions vs emissions reductions
**Publish Date:** December 14, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

A company can sell a product that may help customers avoid emissions while its own corporate inventory is increasing. Those statements are not inherently contradictory because they refer to different boundaries and questions. Confusion begins when avoided-emissions estimates are presented as if they reduce the company’s own inventory total.

Keeping the concepts separate helps teams communicate impact without overstating what a calculation proves.

## Start with the counterfactual

Avoided-emissions estimates compare a product or service scenario with a reference scenario that represents what might otherwise have happened. The result depends on assumptions about the baseline, adoption, use, lifetime, and system boundary. Those assumptions can be highly consequential.

Describe the comparison clearly. If the reference scenario is uncertain or not representative of the customer’s actual alternative, say so.

## Keep the corporate inventory on its own boundary

A corporate greenhouse gas inventory accounts for emissions within its defined organizational and operational boundary using the applicable methodology. A product’s potential effect on a customer’s activity is not automatically a reduction in the company’s Scope 1, 2, or 3 inventory.

Show inventory results and product-impact estimates separately. Do not subtract one from the other unless a recognized accounting method explicitly supports that treatment.

## Check for overlap and double counting

The same claimed benefit could be attributed to a manufacturer, distributor, customer, or other party. Identify who is making the claim, what activity is included, and whether another party may report the same effect. Keep assumptions, units, and time horizon available for review.

A clear limitations section helps stakeholders interpret the number rather than treating it as a verified outcome for every customer.

## Use careful language in public materials

Prefer wording that identifies the estimate, comparison, and boundary. Avoid a single headline such as “carbon negative” when the underlying analysis is a conditional scenario. Have technical and legal reviewers check both the calculation and the claims around it.

**Key Takeaway:** Avoided-emissions estimates describe a modeled comparison; keep them distinct from reductions in the company’s own greenhouse gas inventory.
