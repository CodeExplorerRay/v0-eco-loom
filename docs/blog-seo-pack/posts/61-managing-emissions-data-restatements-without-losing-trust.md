# Managing Emissions Data Restatements Without Losing Trust

Emissions Data Governance | December 25, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Emissions Data Governance
**Primary Keyword:** emissions data restatement
**Publish Date:** December 25, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Restating an emissions figure can feel like an admission that the reporting process failed. In reality, a well-governed correction can improve confidence when new evidence or a material error changes what the organization understands about its past data.

Trust is usually weakened less by a correction than by an unexplained number that changes silently between reports.

## Decide when a correction is required

Create a policy that distinguishes a minor clerical correction from a change that affects comparability, a target, or a public claim. Follow the requirements of the applicable reporting framework and any external commitments. Define who evaluates the issue and who approves a restatement.

A consistent threshold prevents the company from correcting only the changes that make a trend look favorable.

## Preserve the original record

Keep the originally reported value, corrected value, source evidence, calculation, and date of change. Record what caused the issue, which periods and categories are affected, and whether the change was a data error, method update, boundary change, or newly available evidence.

Do not overwrite a value in a way that breaks links to prior reports or reviewer workpapers.

## Assess the effect on comparisons

Determine whether the correction changes year-over-year trends, target progress, or other conclusions. Recalculate related periods when required by the organization’s policy or applicable guidance. If the change is not material to the comparison, document why no broader restatement was made.

Ask an independent reviewer to trace the revised value to its source and verify the explanation.

## Communicate plainly

When a published figure changes, state what changed, why, and which prior period is affected. Keep the explanation proportionate and specific. Avoid vague language such as “methodology refinement” when the underlying issue was a missing facility or duplicate record.

Feed the root cause into the control process so the same issue is less likely to recur.

**Key Takeaway:** A transparent restatement preserves the old and corrected values, explains the cause, and shows how comparability and controls were addressed.
