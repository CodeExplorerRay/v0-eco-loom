# How to Build a Cross-Functional ESG Working Group That Delivers

ESG Governance | December 26, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** ESG Governance
**Primary Keyword:** cross-functional ESG working group
**Publish Date:** December 26, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

An ESG working group can create shared ownership across a company, but only if it makes decisions or removes blockers. A recurring meeting with no defined purpose quickly becomes another status call that participants stop preparing for.

The group should have a clear mandate, a manageable membership, and a direct link to people who can act on its decisions.

## Give the group a specific mandate

Define what the group is responsible for: coordinating data collection, reviewing material topics, escalating risks, or tracking a climate action plan. Clarify what remains with executives, legal, finance, or operational leaders. A short charter can prevent the working group from becoming responsible for every sustainability question.

Tie the mandate to near-term outcomes and the organization’s reporting calendar.

## Choose members for decisions and evidence

Include the functions that own material data and actions, not just people with sustainability in their titles. Depending on the agenda, that may mean operations, procurement, finance, HR, legal, facilities, and communications. Use subject-matter guests when needed rather than making the core group too large.

Name a chair, coordinator, and executive sponsor. Make it clear who can approve actions and who must be consulted.

## Run meetings around decisions and exceptions

Send a short agenda with the records or questions that need review. Focus on unresolved gaps, changing assumptions, upcoming deadlines, and decisions that require cross-functional input. Capture action, owner, due date, and evidence of completion.

Avoid spending meeting time reading dashboards aloud. Share routine status in advance so the group can use its time to solve problems.

## Measure whether the group is useful

Review whether actions close on time, data quality improves, and recurring issues are resolved. Adjust membership or cadence when the work changes. If a topic needs executive authority, escalate it instead of keeping it in the group indefinitely.

**Key Takeaway:** An ESG working group delivers when its mandate is clear, the right data owners participate, and each meeting produces accountable decisions or escalations.
