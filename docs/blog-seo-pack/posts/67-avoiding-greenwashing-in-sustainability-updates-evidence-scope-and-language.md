# Avoiding Greenwashing in Sustainability Updates: Evidence, Scope, and Language

Sustainability Communications | December 28, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** Sustainability Communications
**Primary Keyword:** avoid greenwashing in sustainability updates
**Publish Date:** December 28, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

A sustainability update can overstate progress without anyone intending to mislead. A narrow project result may be described as a company-wide outcome, an estimate may be presented as a measured fact, or a future ambition may sound like an accomplished reduction.

A careful review process makes communications clearer by connecting each statement to evidence, scope, and time period.

## Start with the claim, not the headline

Write each proposed claim as a factual statement and identify what it means. Is it about one site, one product, a specific reporting period, or the whole organization? Does it describe a completed action, a measured outcome, or an intention?

If the claim cannot be made precise in internal language, it is not ready for a public headline.

## Attach evidence to every material statement

Keep the source record, calculation, policy, or project documentation that supports the claim. Assign an owner and reviewer. If the evidence is estimated, partial, or based on a limited sample, capture that context before copywriting compresses it away.

A claim library can store approved wording, boundaries, and expiry or review dates for recurring statements.

## Make qualifications easy to understand

State material limits near the claim, not several pages later. Explain whether figures cover all sites, which emissions scopes are included, and what comparison period is used. Avoid broad terms such as “sustainable” when the evidence supports only a narrower attribute.

Use plain language and test whether a reasonable reader could interpret the statement more broadly than intended.

## Review across functions before publishing

Sustainability or technical teams should validate the method, while legal and communications teams review the wording, context, and intended audience. Keep the decision and final evidence together. Revisit published statements when the underlying data or product changes.

**Key Takeaway:** Sustainability updates are more credible when every claim has a defined scope, period, evidence owner, and language that does not exceed the proof.
