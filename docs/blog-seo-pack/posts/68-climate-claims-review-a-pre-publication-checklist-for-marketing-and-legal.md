# Climate Claims Review: A Pre-Publication Checklist for Marketing and Legal

**Topic:** Climate Claims
**Primary Keyword:** climate claims review checklist
**Publish Date:** December 28, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Climate claims can appear in product pages, annual reports, customer presentations, recruiting materials, and social posts. A statement that is supportable in one context may become misleading when removed from its boundary or explanation.

A pre-publication review should be routine and proportionate, with technical evidence and audience interpretation considered together.

## Define the exact statement and audience

Review the final words, not only the campaign concept. Identify where the claim will appear, who is likely to read it, and what a reasonable reader may infer. Note whether it refers to a product, facility, organization, or future commitment.

A short phrase can imply a broader result than the supporting calculation, so context matters.

## Check scope, period, and method

Confirm which entities, sites, emissions scopes, products, and reporting periods are included. Verify units, calculation methods, assumptions, and factor versions. For a comparative claim, confirm that the baseline and comparison are relevant and consistent.

Keep technical evidence close to the claim and make sure the underlying data has passed its normal review process.

## Test limitations and alternative interpretations

Ask whether an estimate is described as measured, whether a limited project sounds company-wide, or whether future intent is presented as guaranteed. Check that material qualifications are visible and understandable. If the evidence is incomplete, narrow the claim or delay publication until the gap is addressed.

Consider whether the statement could be read as implying an environmental benefit beyond the one actually demonstrated.

## Record approval and monitor changes

Capture technical, legal, and communications review, the evidence relied upon, the final approved version, and any conditions on use. Set a review date when claims rely on data that can change, such as supplier information or product specifications.

**Key Takeaway:** A climate claims checklist should verify exact wording, scope, evidence, limitations, audience interpretation, and approval before publication.
