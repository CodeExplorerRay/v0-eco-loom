# On-Site Solar Emissions: How to Treat Generation and Consumption Data

On-Site Energy | December 30, 2026 | 4 min read

**Topic:** On-Site Energy
**Primary Keyword:** on-site solar emissions reporting
**Publish Date:** December 30, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

On-site solar can change how a facility buys and uses electricity, but the reporting process needs to distinguish generation, consumption, exports, and any contractual attributes. A single production meter or utility bill may not answer every question.

The first step is to map the physical and contractual flows before deciding how each record is represented in the inventory.

## Identify meters and system boundaries

List the solar system, relevant meters, utility connection, and facility load. Confirm which meters measure generation, imports, exports, or net consumption, and note the period each reading covers. Keep equipment identifiers and commissioning dates linked to the site record.

If the facility has multiple tenants or shared services, clarify which operations the data represents.

## Reconcile generation with consumption

Compare generation records with utility invoices and site consumption data. Investigate missing months, meter resets, estimates, and differences between billing periods and calendar periods. Do not assume net consumption is equivalent to total site electricity use if the reporting method requires separate quantities.

Document how exports or shared generation are treated under the applicable methodology.

## Review contractual attributes separately

Ownership of the physical equipment and rights to its renewable attributes may not always be identical. Examine contracts, leases, or financing agreements to understand who can make a claim and what evidence is required. Keep the accounting treatment aligned with current guidance and market rules.

A system installation alone does not settle every claim about renewable electricity use.

## Build an annual evidence packet

Retain meter data, utility statements, system reports, contract terms, and reviewer notes in one location. Record the calculation approach and any assumptions. This packet makes future year comparisons easier and helps explain changes in output or facility demand.

**Key Takeaway:** On-site solar reporting depends on clear meter boundaries and a documented reconciliation of generation, consumption, exports, and contractual rights.
