# ESG for Real Estate: Building Energy, Tenant Data, and Leases

**Topic:** ESG for Real Estate
**Primary Keyword:** ESG for real estate companies
**Publish Date:** December 31, 2026
**Status:** Ready to Publish

Real estate ESG data can be fragmented across property managers, utilities, landlords, tenants, and lease documents. A portfolio total may look simple while hiding gaps in meter coverage, occupancy, operating control, and who can access building information.

The first task is to define the portfolio boundary and make the evidence path visible at each property.

## Build a property and meter register

List owned, leased, managed, and partially occupied properties with stable IDs, address, operating status, and relevant dates. Connect each property to utility meters, property managers, and data owners. Note whether the company pays bills directly or relies on tenant or landlord information.

This register helps identify sites missing from routine data requests.

## Address tenant and landlord data gaps

Define what information is needed, how often, and under what agreement or process it can be shared. Use aggregated consumption where appropriate and protect tenant confidentiality. Record whether a value is measured, estimated, or allocated from a master meter.

If a landlord or tenant cannot provide data, document the gap and a next step rather than silently treating it as zero.

## Connect building performance to investment decisions

Review energy intensity alongside occupancy, floor area, operating hours, and building use. Compare similar properties and make the denominator explicit. Identify opportunities with property teams and track proposed work, investment decisions, and post-implementation evidence separately.

Do not attribute a portfolio-wide improvement to an efficiency project without checking the underlying data and other changes.

## Review leases and boundary changes

Lease renewals, acquisitions, and disposals can affect both control and data access. Establish a review trigger for contract changes and keep prior property lists for comparison. Use qualified guidance to determine how each arrangement is represented in the inventory.

**Key Takeaway:** Real estate ESG reporting depends on a current property and meter map, clear tenant data processes, and documented treatment of lease and control arrangements.
